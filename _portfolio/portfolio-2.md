# Summary
Establishing a sustainable 100,000-person Moon Colony needs a balanced Earth-Moon transportation system. Based on the defined Space Elevator System, this study builds an integrated optimization framework to evaluate diverse logistics strategies for multiple objectives.

For Problem 1, a Cost-Time Trade-off Optimization Model is constructed to characterize the fundamental conflict between transportation cost and project duration under ideal operating conditions. The Pareto frontier is generated through a single-variable discretized exhaustive search over the transportation allocation decision, enabling precise identification of the knee point on the frontier while ensuring global optimality and algorithmic transparency. obtained from the model assigns 74.8% of total materials to the Space Elevator System, completing construction in 139.2 years at a total cost of 271.1 trillion USD, representing a substantial cost reduction relative to the minimum-time strategy with a moderate increase in project duration.

For Problem 2, a Robust Cost-Time Trade-off Optimization Model is developed by incorporating non-ideal operational factors, including Coriolis-force-induced tether constraints, tidal-force-related elevator downtime, and rocket launch failures. These physical and reliability constraints are embedded directly into system capacity estimation, yielding a revised Pareto frontier under realistic uncertainty. The robust knee-point strategy allocates 72.35% of transportation to the space elevator, with a construction period of 164.52 years and a total cost of 296.33 trillion USD. Compared with the ideal-condition solution, the project duration increases by approximately 18.2% and total cost rises by about 9.3%, reflecting the impact of reliability constraints while preserving the dominant role of the space elevator.

For Problem 3, long-term water logistics are formulated as a finite-horizon, multi-stage decision problem. A Dynamic Water Supply Optimization Model, solved using a stage-wise optimization framework, minimizes annual transportation cost while strictly satisfying survival-oriented inventory constraints, ensuring uninterrupted life support after colony establishment. The resulting optimal policy specifies discrete replenishment cycles with a total annual water transportation budget of 500.29 million credits, requiring 17 transportation missions to maintain safe inventory levels.

For Problem 4, environmental consequences are explicitly incorporated through an Environmental Impact-Oriented Decision Model, which introduces an Environmental Impact Coefficient based on radiative forcing equivalence to compare emissions from different transportation strategies. The optimal strategy assigns 88.17% of materials to the Space Elevator System, achieving completion in 89.12 years with a total cost of 141.05 trillion USD, further reinforcing the advantage of space-elevator-dominated logistics.

Finally, for Problem 5, we provide a recommendation letter summarizing the optimal strategy for cost, time, reliability, and environmental objectives.

**Keywords:** Pareto frontier; single-variable discretized exhaustive search; knee-point; stage-wise optimization framework; Environmental Impact Coefficient
